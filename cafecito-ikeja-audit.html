2. The gap, stated plainly

Cafecito already has the hard parts done. You have an active Instagram (@cafecito.ikeja), you trade seven days a week, and your food and space photograph well. Reviewers who find you tend to like you.

The problem is that none of that reaches Google. A customer in Ikeja searching "coffee near me" or "breakfast Ikeja GRA" lands on an unclaimed shell: no number to call, no link to your Instagram, and a single drone photo standing in for your food. The listing reads like a closed business. Meanwhile the places around you — IKOKO, Cilantro, Food Headquarters — carry phone numbers, websites and photo galleries.

The one-line version: you are running a real, active café behind a Google listing that says you might not exist. The fix is not marketing spend. It is 90 minutes of claiming and filling in what you already own.

3. First three fixes, in order

Fix 1 — Claim the listing (highest impact, do this first)

Click "Claim this business" on your Google Maps listing and complete verification. Until this is done, nothing else you fix will stick, because you have no owner control over the profile. Google usually verifies by phone, postcard or video. Everything below depends on this step.

Fix 2 — Fill the three empty fields

Once claimed, add: (a) your phone number, (b) a link — your Instagram profile at minimum, a simple one-page menu site at best, and (c) a second category. Change "Coffee shop" to include "Breakfast restaurant" and "Cafe", so you appear for breakfast and brunch searches, not just coffee. This is roughly 20 minutes of work and it closes the three largest holes a customer sees.

Fix 3 — Put real photos up, then ask for reviews

Upload 15–20 photos: the space, the signature drinks, three or four dishes, the exterior so people recognise it from the road. Replace the drone shot as the cover image with your best food photo. Then ask your regulars for reviews — you already have a 4.0 and every additional honest review lifts the ranking in local search. Photos and review volume are the two signals Google uses to decide who shows up first.
