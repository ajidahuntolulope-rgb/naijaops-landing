2. The gap, stated plainly

Most of the work is already done. You have claimed the listing, your number is on it, your hours are right and you carry a 4.6 rating. That is better than most salons in Ikeja.

What is missing is the link. When a customer in Allen searches "hair salon near me" and taps your listing, they get your name, your number and a rating — and nothing else. No prices, no services list, no photos of your work, no way to see what you do before they call. The customer who is deciding between three salons on that screen has nothing to look at from you.

The one-line version: your listing gets the customer to the phone call, but it gives them no reason to prefer you over the salon listed underneath. One link fixes that, and it takes about 20 minutes.

3. First three fixes, in order

Fix 1 — Put a link on the listing

Open the listing in Google Maps, choose "Add website", and paste a link. If you have no website, your Instagram profile or a free one-page site works. This is the single highest-value change: it turns a name-and-number listing into something a customer can actually evaluate. Roughly 10 minutes.

Fix 2 — Add photos of your work

Upload 10–15 photos: finished styles, the space, the team, the entrance so people recognise it from the road. Google shows the "Add photos & videos" prompt, which tells you the listing is running on no owner media. Salons are bought on visuals. Roughly 30 minutes, and you can do it from your phone between clients.

Fix 3 — Ask your regulars for a review

You have a 4.6 — protect and grow it. The listing has a "Write a review" button and a review count that is not displayed publicly, which usually means it is thin. Ask ten regulars this week for an honest review. Review volume is one of the signals Google uses to decide who appears first in local search, and it compounds.
