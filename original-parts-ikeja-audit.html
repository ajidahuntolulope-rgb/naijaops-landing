2. The gap, stated plainly

A car owner with a broken part searches on their phone, finds your listing, and sees a name and a number. For a spare-parts shop that is the entire decision, and it is not enough. They do not know whether you stock their make, whether you have the part today, or whether you are worth the drive to Opebi. The shops they are comparing you against are on the same screen.

The one-line version: a customer with a broken car will call whoever they believe has the part. Your listing gives them no reason to believe it is you. One link changes that.

3. First three fixes, in order

Fix 1 — Put a link on the listing

Open the listing in Google Maps, choose "Add website", and paste a link. A one-page list of the makes you cover and how to reach you is enough to start. About ten minutes.

Fix 2 — Add photos of stock and the shopfront

Upload shelves, the counter and the entrance so a customer recognises the place from the road and can see you actually carry stock. Spare-parts buyers judge a shop on what they can see. You can do this from your phone.

Fix 3 — Ask buyers for reviews

The listing has a "Write a review" button. Ask customers who found the right part to leave an honest review.
